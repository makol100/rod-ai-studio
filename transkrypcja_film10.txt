AI agents using this rich structural context catch issues that even experienced human reviewers routinely miss, without the false positive noise of traditional linters. Setup requires less than five minutes of your time. Point Code Review Graph at your repository root directory, run the indexing command, and within seconds you have a fully queryable structural graph of your entire project. MCP integration means any AI agent using the model context protocol gains instant access to your project's full architectural understanding with zero additional configuration. Coming up at number six, a project with an unusual name. Number six is I Have ADHD by Ayghri, and its name tells you exactly what it does. 11,000 total stars and nearly 7,000 stars added this week. This is a skill for your coding agent that stops it from burying the answer in walls of irrelevant text. ADHD-friendly output that respects your attention as a finite resource. The problem is universally recognized by every developer who has used AI assistance. You ask a straightforward question and receive a multi-paragraph essay back. The information you actually need is somewhere in that text block, but finding it costs you minutes of careful scrolling and scanning. I Have ADHD solves this by constraining AI output strictly to what actually matters. The approach is deceptively simple. The skill enforces four rules on any AI agent. Lead with the answer, never with the reasoning you did not ask for. Use bullet points instead of paragraphs. Never repeat information already communicated. And when a single sentence suffices, stop there. No fluff, no filler, no unnecessary introductions. Built as a Python-based skill for AI coding agents, it integrates seamlessly with Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Cursor, and any tool that supports system prompt customization. The MIT license means you can adapt it to your specific workflow without any restriction whatsoever. Over 500 forks already suggest a lot of developers are doing exactly that and sharing improvements. Measurable impact on developer productivity is significant. When AI responses are genuinely concise and direct, developers actually read them completely instead of skimming. Decision time drops from minutes of parsing to seconds of reading. The cognitive load of filtering verbose output vanishes entirely, leaving your mental bandwidth free to focus on the actual engineering problem at hand. What makes this project particularly noteworthy is its underlying philosophy. It treats AI attention management as a genuine design problem, rather than a technical limitation. By acknowledging that how information is presented matters, just as much as the information content itself, it challenges the entire paradigm of how we think about human-AI interaction and communication design.
